    <h2>
    1: The Legal Architecture of the Beckham Law Spain 2026
</h2>

    <p>
        The Special Tax Regime for Displaced Workers, globally known as the <strong>Beckham Law (Article 93 of the Spanish Personal Income Tax Act)</strong>, has undergone a radical transformation. In 2026, it is no longer a niche benefit for elite athletes—who are now notably excluded—but a sophisticated tool for <strong>Wealth Architecture</strong> designed to attract tech equity, scientific talent, and digital sovereignty.
    </p>

    <h3>1.1 The «Nexus of Causality»: The 2026 Movement Rule</h3>
    <p>
        Article 93.1.b requires relocation as a <em>consequence</em> of one of its specified circumstances, in the first tax year under the regime or the preceding year. The <strong>causal connection</strong> and supporting dates must be established for the actual route; there is no universal qualification based simply on holding a visa, a degree or three years of experience. For the entrepreneurial route in Article 93.1.b.3, Article 113.2 RIRPF requires an innovative activity and/or one of special economic interest with a favourable ENISA report, together with the applicable pre-relocation residence authorisation; EU/free-movement applicants follow the specific pre-relocation ENISA-report route. This is distinct from the highly qualified professional route in Article 93.1.b.4.
    </p>

    <h3>1.2 The «5-Year Non-Residency» Threshold</h3>
    <p>
        The 2026 update consolidates the <strong>Startup Act&#8217;s reduction</strong> of the previous non-residency period from 10 years to just <strong>5 tax years</strong>. This is a critical SEO entity: <em>«Fiscal disconnection»</em>. To be eligible, the applicant must not have been a tax resident in Spain during the five years preceding their arrival. 
    </p>
    <blockquote>
        «A tax year in Spain is the calendar year. Even if you lived in Spain for 184 days in 2020, that counts as a full year of residency, potentially resetting your 5-year clock.»
    </blockquote>

    <h3>1.3 Exclusions: The «Professional Athlete» &#038; Passive Income Trap</h3>
    <p>
        It is a common misconception that anyone with a high salary qualifies. In 2026, the AEAT is strictly filtering out:
    </p>
    <ul>
        <li>
            <strong>Professional Athletes:</strong> Still excluded under the post-2015 amendments to avoid «sporting tax havens.»
        </li>
        <li>
            <strong>Company Directors:</strong> Under Article 93.1.b.2 LIRPF, the shareholding restriction applies where the entity is patrimonial, by reference to related-party status under Article 18 LIS. It is not a general restriction on every non-innovative business, and passive investment alone is not a qualifying relocation route.
        </li>
    </ul>

    <h3>1.4 The 183-Day Residency Paradox</h3>
    <p>
        Under the Beckham Law, you remain an <strong>IRPF taxpayer</strong>, with special calculation rules based on non-resident taxation. There is no blanket exemption for foreign receipts: Article 93.2 treats employment income and income from qualifying entrepreneurial activities during the regime as Spanish-source. Article 114.2.a RIRPF excludes activities before relocation or after the end-of-relocation notification from that rule, without removing any tax due under Spanish-source rules. Other income requires separate classification and sourcing. The regime applies in the year Spanish tax residence is acquired and the next five, subject to Article 115 RIRPF and continued compliance.
    </p>

  <h2 id="chapter-2">
    2: Eligibility Criteria for Beckham Law Spain 2026
</h2>
<p>
    Not everyone can apply for this regime. The <strong>Beckham Law Spain 2026</strong> requirements are strict: you must not have been a tax resident in Spain during the last 5 years. This «5-year rule» is the cornerstone of the <strong>Beckham Law Spain 2026</strong> eligibility, ensuring that only new residents benefit from the tax shield.
</p>

    <h3>2.1 Real Obligation vs. Personal Obligation</h3>
    <p>
        Under <em>personal obligation</em>, <strong>Wealth Tax (IP)</strong> concerns worldwide net wealth, subject to the applicable exemptions, valuation, allowances and filing rules. Article 93 LIRPF places a qualifying special-regime taxpayer under <strong>real obligation</strong>: the relevant scope is assets and rights situated, exercisable or enforceable in Spain, applying Article 5 of Law 19/1991. Tax liability and filing duties must be calculated separately; no universal regional rate or personal exemption threshold is assumed here. 
    </p>
    <ul>
        <li>
            <strong>Standard Resident:</strong> Worldwide net wealth falls within personal-obligation scope, but the tax due and filing duty depend on exemptions, valuation and the applicable territorial rules, not a universal percentage of total assets.
        </li>
        <li>
            <strong>Beckham Resident:</strong> Article 93 provides real-obligation Wealth Tax treatment. Check legal location and enforceability, not merely the custodian&#8217;s country: Article 5 of Law 19/1991 includes a Spanish-location rule for certain unlisted interests in entities whose assets are predominantly Spanish real estate. Foreign ownership structures do not establish an automatic exemption.
        </li>
    </ul>

    <h3>2.2 The «Solidarity Tax» Trap (ITSGF) in 2026</h3>
    <p>
        The <em>Impuesto Temporal de Solidaridad de las Grandes Fortunas</em> (ITSGF) is governed by Article 3 of Law 38/2022 and has been extended pending reform of wealth taxation. Its reference to net wealth above <strong>€3,000,000</strong> is not a universal threshold at which everyone must pay or file: taxable scope, exempt assets, the allowance, the zero-rate band, Wealth Tax actually paid and other applicable rules affect the result. Under Article 3.Nineteen, filing depends on a resulting amount payable. This guide does not calculate territorial or foral liabilities.
    </p>
    <p>
        <strong>Scope of the rules:</strong> Article 3.Five of Law 38/2022 identifies ITSGF taxpayers by reference to Wealth Tax and in the same terms. Read this with Article 93 LIRPF and Article 5 of Law 19/1991; it does not change the taxpayer into an ordinary IRNR taxpayer or exempt every asset described as foreign. Determine the assets within scope and the separate filing outcome.
    </p>

That statement is not a blanket exemption for every relative or every reporting form: assess each person&#8217;s own status. Form 721 concerns certain virtual currencies held abroad and requires a separate check of taxpayer status, custody, location, thresholds and exceptions.     <p>
        Article 95 bis LIRPF concerns gains on qualifying shares or participations when tax residence changes, subject to the ten-of-fifteen-period condition, value or ownership thresholds and the applicable exceptions. For former Beckham-regime taxpayers, Article 95 bis.8 expressly starts the ten-period count in the first tax period in which the special regime no longer applies. An individual review is still required before a move.

    <h2>
        4: The Family Unit Extension — Multiplicating 24% Efficiency
    </h2>

    <p>
        One of the most profound shifts in the 2026 tax landscape is the maturity of the <strong>Family Unit Extension</strong>. Historically, the Beckham Law was an individual privilege; today, it is a household strategy. However, the Spanish Tax Agency (AEAT) has implemented a «Satellite Requirement» system where the family’s eligibility is strictly tethered to the main applicant’s compliance.
    </p>

    <h3>4.1 Eligible Relatives &#038; the First Tax Period</h3>
    <p>
        Not all relatives qualify. In 2026, the law is surgical about who can join the 24% flat rate regime:
    </p>
    <ul>
        <li>
            <strong>Spouse:</strong> Article 93.3 covers the spouse. A registered partnership alone does not automatically confer eligibility.
        </li>
        <li>
            <strong>Children:</strong> Under 25 years of age, or any age if they have a certified disability.
        </li>
        <li>
            <strong>No Marital Bond:</strong> The parent of those children may qualify where there is no marital bond, subject to all Article 93.3 conditions.
        </li>
    </ul>
    <p>
        ⚠️ Relatives may move with the principal or later, before the end of the principal&#8217;s first tax period under the regime, not within a universal rolling 12 months. Article 113.3 RIRPF also permits an earlier move if they do not acquire tax residence before the principal&#8217;s first tax period under the regime. They must acquire Spanish tax residence and meet the five-period prior non-residence and permanent-establishment conditions in Article 93.3.
    </p>

    <h3>4.2 The «Taxable Base» Cap: The Ultimate Hurdle</h3>
    <p>
        This is the detail where most DIY applications fail. To prevent tax erosion, the 2026 jurisprudence maintains a strict financial ceiling:
    </p>
    
        <p>«The combined bases liquidables of the family applicants <strong>must be lower than</strong> the principal applicant&#8217;s base liquidable in each period under Article 93.3.d LIRPF. Equality does not qualify.»</p>
        <p>
            Example: if the principal&#8217;s base liquidable is €100,000 and the relatives&#8217; combined bases liquidables are €120,000, the family-extension condition is <strong>not met</strong>. Compare the statutory bases, not gross salaries. An independent qualifying route requires separate assessment.
        </p>

    <h3>5.1 Stock Options &#038; RSUs: The «Sourcing» Trap</h3>
    <p>
        RSUs and stock options require analysis of the <strong>activity remunerated</strong>, the dates and the sourcing rules. Articles 93.2 LIRPF and 114.2.a RIRPF distinguish income during the regime from income linked to activity before relocation or after the end-of-relocation notification. A foreign employer does not establish an automatic exemption. 
    </p>
    <ul>
        <li>
            <strong>Pre-Relocation Vesting:</strong> If options vested entirely while you were working outside Spain, the income is generally not taxable in Spain, even if exercised while resident, subject to sourcing rules and specific factual circumstances.
        </li>
        <li>
            <strong>Post-Relocation Activity:</strong> Where the award is taxable employment income under the regime, the relevant base is taxed at <strong>24% up to €600,000 and 47% above that threshold</strong>. Vesting or exercise dates alone do not settle every cross-border allocation question.
        </li>

    <h3>5.2 The 2026 Crypto Frontier: Taxation of Digital Assets</h3>

    <h2>
        6: Life After Beckham — The «Year 7» Transition
    </h2>
    <p>
        The Beckham Law is a finite privilege. In 2026, the «Year 7 Trap» has become a major concern for expats who have built a life in Spain. After the end of the special regime period, you revert to the <strong>Standard Progressive Tax Regime</strong>. Without advance planning, your effective tax rate may increase significantly compared to the 24% flat rate under the special regime.
    </p>

    <h3>6.1 The 10-Year Exit Tax Threshold</h3>
    <p>
        One of the most technical aspects of Spanish tax law is the <strong>Exit Tax (Impuesto de Salida)</strong>. Under current regulations, if you have been a tax resident in Spain for 10 out of the last 15 years, moving abroad may trigger a tax on unrealized capital gains on certain qualifying assets, subject to thresholds and exemptions.
    </p>
    
        <h4>The «Beckham Shield» Consideration:</h4>
        <p>
            Article 95 bis.8 LIRPF states that, for taxpayers who used the special regime, the ten-tax-period count begins in the first period in which that regime no longer applies. This timing rule is distinct from the asset thresholds, valuation, deferral and other conditions governing a specific exit.
        </p>
    

    <h3>6.2 Strategic Deferral of Income</h3>
