## Introduction: What is the Spain Digital Nomad Visa?

The Spain Digital Nomad Visa is a residence authorization designed for remote workers and freelancers engaged in international remote work. It was created by **Law 28/2022, of December 21, on promoting the emerging business ecosystem** (known as the "Startup Law"), which amended Law 14/2013 on support for entrepreneurs, introducing article 74.2.bis into the Foreign Nationals Act.

This visa recognizes that remote work enables professionals of any nationality to work for companies and clients based abroad while residing legally in Spain, without requiring a local employment contract or a Spanish company to hire them. It is a modern and flexible pathway to attract international talent in sectors such as technology, consulting, design, marketing, and other professional services.

## Who Can Apply for the Spain Digital Nomad Visa?

The visa is aimed at two primary profiles:

**Remote employees:** Workers who maintain an employment relationship (employment contract) with a foreign company and perform their work remotely from Spain.

**Self-employed professionals:** Independent professionals who invoice foreign clients and generate income from internationally delivered services.

**Important restriction for self-employed:** If you are self-employed, you must ensure that **at least 80% of your income comes from foreign clients**. The visa does not permit activity primarily oriented toward the Spanish market; if you have Spanish clients but the majority of income is from abroad, this is considered a risk factor in your application evaluation and should be confirmed with the consulate.

## Digital Nomad Visa Requirements

The Digital Nomad Visa requires you to meet a specific set of requirements:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **No prior tax residency** | You must not have been a tax resident in Spain in the last 5 years. Note: the eligibility checker applies this requirement; it is advisable to confirm it directly with the consulate. |
| **International remote work** | Work for a foreign company or foreign clients (≥80% of income from abroad for self-employed). Written employment contract or documented business relationship. |
| **Minimum income** | Gross annual income ≥200% of the Interprofessional Minimum Wage (SMI). The SMI is updated annually; in 2026 the approximate figure is €2,400/month, but check the current value at the time of your application. |
| **Experience or qualification** | Hold a university degree (Bachelor or higher), OR demonstrate more than 3 years of professional experience in your sector. |
| **Prior contract or relationship** | Evidence of a minimum employment or business relationship (generally of at least 1 year of tenure, according to case law). |
| **Employer minimum activity** | If you are an employee, the employing company must have minimum real activity of 1 year. |
| **Health insurance** | Comprehensive private health coverage in Spain (you do not need a history, but it must be in force before applying). |
| **Clean criminal record** | No criminal convictions in Spain or in countries of residence during the last 5 years. |

## Dependents and Family Members

If you wish to bring your spouse or common-law partner, or your minor children, you can include them in your application as dependents. Required income increases:

- **First dependent:** increase of 75% of the SMI.
- **Each additional dependent:** increase of 25% of the SMI.

For example, if your spouse and child apply for the visa with you, you would need annual income of 200% SMI + 75% SMI (spouse) + 25% SMI (child) = **300% SMI**.

## Two Application Routes: Consulate vs. UGE-CE

There are two main paths to obtain the Spain Digital Nomad Visa:

### Route 1: Visa at Spanish Consulate (from abroad)
- **Duration:** 1 year (renewable).
- **Procedure:** In-person application at the Spanish consulate in your country of residence.
- **Timeline:** Varies by consulate; allow several weeks.
- **Requirement:** You must be outside Spain at the time of application.

### Route 2: Residence Authorization from Spain (UGE-CE)
- **Duration:** 3 years (renewable for an additional 2 years).
- **Procedure:** Application through the Unit for Large Companies and Strategic Collectives (UGE-CE) if you are already in Spain (for example, on a short-stay visa or as a tourist).
- **Timeline:** 20 business days with "tacit approval" (if you receive no response within that period, your application is considered approved).
- **Advantage:** Faster and does not require traveling to the consulate again.

The choice depends on your situation: if you are outside Spain, choose the consulate route; if you are already inside, the UGE-CE route is more efficient.

## Renewal and Long-Term Residence Access

The Spain Digital Nomad Visa is renewable:

- **First renewal:** 2 years.
- **Subsequent renewals:** depend on the initial format (1 year, 3 years, 2 years depending on the route chosen).

**Long-Term Residence:** After 5 years of continuous residence in Spain with the Digital Nomad Visa (or combining other types of authorization), you can apply for **Long-Term Residence**. This is a higher category that offers greater stability and facilitates access to other rights (such as local employment, if you wish to change your situation).

## Tax Benefits: Regime for Non-Residents (Beckham Law)

A significant advantage for the Spain Digital Nomad Visa is the option to qualify for the **special tax regime for inbound workers** ("Beckham Law"), regulated in article 93 of the Personal Income Tax Law.

**Benefit:** Reduced taxation of **24% up to €600,000 of gross annual income** (instead of the ordinary progressive income tax, which can reach 45%).

**Key requirements:**
- You must not have been a tax resident in Spain in the 10 years before applying.
- You must have a work or economic connection to Spain (which your Digital Nomad Visa provides).

## Required Documentation

To apply for the Spain Digital Nomad Visa, you will need to submit:

- **Valid passport:** Valid for at least 1 year from the date of application.
- **Employment contract or business relationship:** Original and copy, in English or certified Spanish translation if not in Spanish.
- **Proof of income:** Recent payslips, income declaration, or bank statements demonstrating income ≥200% of the SMI.
- **Curriculum vitae:** Detailing your professional experience.
- **Criminal record certificate:** From your country of origin and all countries where you have resided in the last 5 years (legalized with Apostille certification).
- **Health insurance policy:** Comprehensive private health coverage in Spain.
- **Proof of address:** Rental contract, property ownership certificate, or similar.
- **Proof of qualification or experience:** University diploma and/or professional certificates.
- **Application form:** Available on the consulate or UGE-CE website.

## Timelines and Common Mistakes

### Processing times
- **Consulate route:** varies by consulate (allow several weeks).
- **UGE-CE route from Spain:** 20 business days (tacit approval).

### Mistakes to avoid
1. **Confusing net income with gross income:** The requirement is 200% of the SMI **gross**; you do not deduct taxes or social contributions.
2. **Failing to adequately document the employment relationship:** Ensure that your contract is signed by a legal representative of the company and is verifiable.
3. **Not verifying the percentage of foreign income:** For self-employed, keep records of invoices by country and ensure ≥80% from abroad.
4. **Delaying health insurance procurement:** Obtain the policy well in advance; some consulates require 30 days of validity.
5. **Changing employers between application and approval:** Notify the consulate or UGE-CE of any changes.
6. **Failing to update the SMI:** Required income varies annually; verify the current SMI in the year of your application.

## Brief Comparison: Digital Nomad vs. Highly Qualified Professional

| Aspect | Digital Nomad | Highly Qualified Professional |
|---|---|---|
| **Employer** | Foreign company/client | Spanish company |
| **Minimum income** | 200% of SMI (~€2,400/month) | €40,000–€54,000 annually depending on category |
| **Experience/qualification** | 3 years OR university degree | University degree or 3 years in strategic role |
| **Processing time (UGE-CE)** | 20 business days | 20 business days |
| **Initial duration** | 1 year (consulate) or 3 years (UGE-CE) | 3 years |
| **Beckham Law eligibility** | Yes, if requirements met | Yes, if requirements met |
| **Career transition** | Can switch to local employment | Must have local contract from the start |

The Digital Nomad Visa is more flexible if you work remotely; the Highly Qualified Professional is better if you have a job offer in Spain.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Can I work for a Spanish company if it is only 20% of my income?**
Yes, as a self-employed, as long as at least 80% of your income is from abroad. If you wish to work primarily for a Spanish company, consider the Highly Qualified Professional Visa.

**Q: Do I need the exact 2026 SMI or is it approximate?**
The SMI is updated annually. The checker uses the current value. Verify the official SMI of the year in which you apply (normally published in January). The figure of "~€2,400/month" is indicative for 2026; use the official figure.

**Q: What happens if my income falls below 200% of the SMI after obtaining the visa?**
The income requirement is evaluated at the time of application. Once approved, there is no automatic annual review. However, at renewal, you will need to prove that you still meet the requirements.

**Q: Does the visa include the right to work locally in Spain?**
Not directly. The Digital Nomad Visa authorizes international remote work. If you want local employment, you should apply for a change of authorization (for example, to Highly Qualified Professional) or a modification of situation.

**Q: Can I apply for the visa while legally residing in Spain (e.g., on another visa)?**
Yes, through the UGE-CE route. However, if you were registered as a tax resident in Spain less than 5 years ago, this may be a factor to review; consult with the consulate or UGE-CE.
