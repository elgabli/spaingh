**Quick answer.** Most foreigners obtain Spanish citizenship **by residence**: 10 years of legal, continuous residence as a general rule, **2 years** for nationals of Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea and Portugal (and people of Sephardic origin), **5 years** for refugees and **1 year** for spouses of Spanish citizens, people born in Spain and children or grandchildren of Spaniards. On top of the years you need a clean criminal record, the CCSE civic exam and, unless Spanish is an official language of your country, the DELE A2 language exam. The application is filed online with the Ministry of Justice, costs about €105 in fees, and is decided within a legal deadline of one year (12–18 months in practice). There is **no citizenship by investment** in Spain. Use the checker above to see which route applies to you.

Spanish citizenship is not something you apply for when you arrive. It is the end of a period of **legal residence in Spain**, and everything else — the language exam, the file, the oath — comes after those years have been completed. This guide sets out the routes, the requirements, the documents, the costs and the realistic timeline, based on article 22 of the Spanish Civil Code and the nationality regulations applied by the Ministry of Justice. If you have not yet secured legal residence, start with the routes that lead there: the [Highly Qualified Professional visa](/en/highly-qualified-professional-visa-spain/), the [Digital Nomad Visa](/en/spain-digital-nomad-visa/) or the options described in our guide to [moving to Spain from the UK](/en/how-easy-is-it-to-move-to-spain-from-uk/).

## The four ways to become Spanish

| Route | Who | Residence required |
|---|---|---|
| **By origin** | Children of a Spanish mother or father; children born in Spain when the parents' law does not give them a nationality | None: the nationality is declared by registering the birth |
| **By option** | Children of people who were originally Spanish; people born abroad to Spanish-born parents or grandparents (within the Civil Code deadlines) | None or 1 year, depending on the case |
| **By residence** | Everyone else: 10, 5, 2 or 1 year of legal residence | See the periods below |
| **By naturalisation letter** | Exceptional, discretionary grant by the Government (sportspeople, victims of terrorism, exceptional circumstances) | Not a route you can plan for |

This guide focuses on citizenship **by residence**, the route that applies to the vast majority of expatriates, investors and professionals living in Spain.

## Residence permit vs. citizenship

| | Residence permit (TIE) | Spanish citizenship |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Authorisation to live in Spain, temporary or long-term | Full membership of the Spanish state |
| Renewal | Yes (temporary permits); long-term permit renewed as a card every 5 years | Never expires |
| Passport | No. You keep your own passport | Spanish and EU passport, free movement and work in all EU countries |
| Vote | No (except municipal elections for some nationalities) | Yes, all elections |
| Loss | Can be lost through prolonged absence | Only in exceptional cases |

Legal residence is the foundation. Citizenship by residence is only available to people who have held a residence authorisation, not a tourist stay and, as a rule, not a student stay.

## The residence periods: 10, 5, 2 or 1 year

Article 22 of the Civil Code sets the period of legal, continuous residence immediately before the application:

| Period | Who |
|---|---|
| **10 years** | General rule. Applies to US, UK, Canadian, Australian, Chinese, Indian, Moroccan and most other nationals, including EU citizens. |
| **5 years** | People who have been granted refugee status in Spain. |
| **2 years** | Nationals by origin of Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea and Portugal, and people of Sephardic origin. |
| **1 year** | Born in Spanish territory; married to a Spanish citizen for at least one year and not legally or de facto separated; widow or widower of a Spanish citizen; born outside Spain to a parent or grandparent who was originally Spanish; people who did not exercise the right to opt in time; people under the guardianship of a Spanish citizen or institution for two consecutive years. |

### How long by nationality

| Your nationality | Years of residence | Keep your original passport? |
|---|---|---|
| Argentina, Colombia, Mexico, Venezuela, Peru, Chile and the rest of Ibero-America | 2 | Yes |
| Philippines, Portugal, Andorra, Equatorial Guinea | 2 | Yes |
| United States, Canada, Australia | 10 | Renunciation declared before the Civil Registry; effect depends on your country's law |
| United Kingdom, Ireland | 10 | Renunciation declared; the UK does not treat the Spanish declaration as loss of British citizenship |
| France | 10 | Yes, under the 2021 Spain–France dual nationality agreement |
| Germany, Italy, Netherlands and other EU countries | 10 | Renunciation declared; effect depends on your country's law |
| Morocco, China, India, Pakistan, Russia | 10 | Renunciation declared |

Three conditions apply to every period:

1. **Legal.** You must have held a valid residence authorisation for the whole period. Time spent as a tourist does not count. Time on a student stay (*estancia por estudios*) does not count towards nationality either, although since the 2025 immigration regulation it counts at 50% towards long-term residence. We explain the difference in [Does a student visa count towards residency in Spain?](/en/does-student-visa-count-towards-residency-spain/)
2. **Continuous.** The period must not be interrupted. The law does not fix a maximum number of days abroad; in practice, the courts accept short trips and treat absences of more than six months in a row, or more than a year in total over the period, as a break in continuity. Keep trips short and keep evidence of your life in Spain (employment, lease, children's schooling, tax returns).
3. **Immediately before the application.** You must still be a legal resident when you apply and while the file is processed.

## Who applies through marriage or descent

**Marriage to a Spanish citizen** reduces the period to one year, but the year only starts once the marriage is registered in the Spanish Civil Registry. A marriage celebrated abroad must be registered at the consulate or in Spain before it counts. You must also be living together in Spain during that year.

**Children of a Spanish parent** usually do not need residence at all. If your mother or father was Spanish when you were born, you are Spanish by origin and simply need to register the birth. If the parent acquired Spanish nationality later, or you were born outside Spain to Spanish-born parents or grandparents, you may be able to **opt** for nationality within the deadlines set by the Civil Code, or apply after one year of residence.

**Grandchildren and descendants of exiles**: the Democratic Memory Law (Ley 20/2022) opened a special window for descendants of Spaniards who lost their nationality through exile. That window closed in October 2025. Descendants who did not apply in time are now back to the general rules above.

**Sephardic Jews**: the special route of Law 12/2015 closed in October 2019. People of Sephardic origin now apply by residence, with the reduced two-year period.

## There is no Spanish citizenship by investment

Spain has never sold passports. The investor residence permit known as the *Golden Visa* only granted **residence**, and that time counted towards the ordinary 10-year period like any other permit. The Golden Visa was abolished on 3 April 2025 (Organic Law 1/2025). Investors who want to settle in Spain today use the same permits as everyone else — [Digital Nomad Visa](/en/services/digital-nomad-visa-spain/), [Highly Qualified Professional](/en/services/highly-qualified-professional-visa/), self-employed or non-lucrative residence — and then the residence periods above.

## Requirements common to every applicant

- **Legal residence** for the required period, as explained.
- **Good civic conduct.** Criminal record certificates from Spain and from every country where you have lived in the last five years, legalised or apostilled and translated. Minor offences can lead to refusal; check whether old records can be cancelled before you apply.
- **Integration into Spanish society**, proven through two exams run by the Instituto Cervantes:
  - **CCSE** (constitutional and sociocultural knowledge): 25 multiple-choice questions on the Constitution, institutions, geography and culture. The question bank is published in advance and the pass rate is very high with a few hours of preparation.
  - **DELE A2** or a higher Spanish language diploma. Nationals of countries where Spanish is an official language are exempt from the DELE. Applicants who completed compulsory secondary education in Spain, people with reading and writing difficulties and minors have full or partial exemptions.
- **Age**: applicants must be over 18, or over 14 with parental assistance. Parents apply on behalf of younger children.

## Documents

The standard file for citizenship by residence includes:

- Application form and payment of the fee (model 790, code 026).
- Valid passport and Spanish residence card (TIE) for the whole period, including previous cards.
- Birth certificate from your country of origin, legalised or apostilled, with sworn translation.
- Criminal record certificates from your country of origin and any country of residence in the last five years, and from Spain.
- Certificate of registration at the town hall (*padrón*) showing your address history.
- CCSE and DELE certificates or proof of exemption.
- Proof of means of subsistence: employment contracts, tax returns, self-employment registration or pension income.
- For the one-year marriage route: Spanish marriage certificate from the Civil Registry, spouse's Spanish ID and *padrón* showing shared address.

Foreign documents have a limited validity once issued, so order them in the right sequence: criminal records and birth certificates last, once the rest of the file is ready.

| Item | Approximate cost |
|---|---|

| CCSE exam | €85 |
| DELE A2 exam | €130–150 depending on the centre |
| Apostilles and legalisations | €20–80 per document, depending on the country |
| Sworn translations | €30–60 per page |
| Criminal record certificates | Free to €40 depending on the country |
| DNI and passport after approval | About €12 + €30 |

Legal fees vary with the complexity of the file (number of countries of residence, family members, previous refusals). A well-prepared file costs less than a refusal followed by an appeal.

## How to apply, step by step

1. **Check your period and continuity.** Count from the date of your first residence card, not from your arrival or your NIE.
2. **Pass the CCSE and, if required, the DELE A2.** Exams are held several times a year; book early because places in large cities fill up.
3. **Gather and legalise the documents.** Apostille for Hague Convention countries, consular legalisation for the rest; sworn translation into Spanish.
4. **Submit the application online** through the Ministry of Justice electronic platform. Online filing has been the standard route since 2016 and is faster than filing on paper at the Civil Registry. A lawyer or authorised representative can file on your behalf.
5. **Wait for the decision.** The legal deadline is one year; in practice, files are being resolved in 12 to 18 months in 2025–2026, after the backlog of previous years was cleared. If there is no answer after a year, the application is deemed refused by administrative silence, which opens the way to an appeal — most applicants simply wait for the express decision.
6. **Oath and registration.** Once approved, you have 180 days to attend the Civil Registry, swear or promise loyalty to the King and obedience to the Constitution, and, where required, renounce your previous nationality. Your Spanish nationality is registered on that day.
7. **DNI and passport.** With the registration certificate you can apply for your Spanish ID card and passport at the National Police.

## Dual nationality

Spain does not allow dual nationality as a general rule. Adults acquiring citizenship by residence must declare that they renounce their previous nationality, **except** nationals of Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea, Portugal and, since 2022, France, who keep both.

For citizens of the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany and most other countries, the renunciation is a declaration made before the Spanish Civil Registry. Whether that declaration causes the loss of your original nationality depends on the law of that country, not on Spanish law. Take advice in your country of origin before the oath if keeping your original passport matters to you.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to get Spanish citizenship?
Add the residence period (10, 5, 2 or 1 year) to the processing time (12–18 months in practice) and a few months to obtain the exams and documents. A Latin American professional typically holds a Spanish passport 3 to 4 years after arriving; a US or UK citizen, 11 to 12 years.

### How much does Spanish citizenship cost?
The official fee is about €105, plus €85 for the CCSE exam and €130–150 for the DELE A2 if you are not exempt. Apostilles, translations and certificates add a few hundred euros. Legal fees depend on the file.

### Does time on a student visa count?
Not for nationality. Student stays count at 50% towards long-term residence under the 2025 regulation, but the nationality period starts with your first residence authorisation.

### Can I get Spanish citizenship by buying property or investing?
No. Spain has no citizenship-by-investment programme. The investor Golden Visa, abolished in April 2025, only granted residence; investors follow the same residence periods as everyone else.

### Can I apply from abroad?
No. Citizenship by residence requires you to be legally resident in Spain at the time of the application and during processing.

### How long can I spend outside Spain during the period?
There is no fixed limit in the law. Short trips are not a problem. Absences of more than six months in a row, or of more than a year in total, break continuity and lead to refusal.

### Can I keep my US or British passport?
Spanish law requires you to declare renunciation before the Civil Registry. Neither the United States nor the United Kingdom treats that declaration as an effective loss of their citizenship, so in practice many people keep both passports. This is a matter of your country's law, not of Spanish law; take advice before the oath.

### Can my children apply with me?
Minor children who are legal residents can apply through their parents and benefit from the same period. Children born in Spain to foreign parents can apply after one year of legal residence.

### What happens if the application is refused?
Refusals can be appealed before the Ministry of Justice and then before the administrative courts. Common reasons are absences, criminal records and missing or expired documents, so most refusals are avoidable with a properly prepared file.
