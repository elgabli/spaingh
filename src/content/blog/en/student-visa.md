  <p><strong>Does a student visa count towards residency in Spain?</strong> In Spanish immigration law, the answer to this question depends on a critical distinction between <strong>Estancia</strong> (Stay) and <strong>Residencia</strong> (Residency). Since the new Immigration Law Regulations came into effect in 2025, applications to reside and work in Spain have increased by nearly 50%, underscoring the need for technical precision when planning your legal future. Student visas (Visado de Estudiante) are strictly classified as <strong>Estancia por Estudios</strong>, meaning you are authorized to stay for a specific academic purpose without being considered a legal resident for most long-term legal effects. Under the updated 2026 regime, these permits now cover the entire duration of the academic program, aligning with broader reforms designed to streamline residency pathways for international talent.</p>

    <h2>Does Student Time Count for Residency &#038; Citizenship?</h2>
    <p><strong>Time spent in Spain under a student visa does not count towards Spanish citizenship and does not constitute full legal residency for long-term residence purposes.</strong> This is because student stay is legally classified as «estancia» rather than «residencia» under Spanish immigration law and the Civil Code. For nationality (naturalization) purposes, only uninterrupted periods of legal residence (residencia) are considered valid.</p>

    <ul>
        <li>
            <strong>EU Long-Term Residency:</strong> For the EU Long-Term Residency permit, <a href="https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2011-7703#a148" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Article 148 of Royal Decree 557/2011</a> establishes that only 50% of the time spent under student status may be counted towards the required five-year period, provided the applicant holds a subsequent residence permit at the time of application. The remaining time must be accumulated under a residence authorization. 
        </li>
        <li>
            <strong>Spanish Citizenship:</strong> According to <a href="https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1889-4763#a22" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Article 22 of the Spanish Civil Code</a>, only periods of legal residence («residencia legal») are valid for nationality purposes. Periods under student status («estancia por estudios») are entirely excluded from the general 10-year residence requirement for naturalization. 
        </li>
    </ul>

    
        <strong>May 2025 Reform (Reloex):</strong> Following the 2025 reform of the Immigration Regulations (<a href="https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2024-24099" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Royal Decree 1155/2024</a>), which entered into force on 20 May 2025, student stay permits now permit employment of up to <strong>30 hours per week</strong>, and the transition from a study permit to a work/residence authorization is streamlined. Additionally, the reform unifies and expands multiple pathways for residence (including family reunification and other categories), facilitating longer-term planning for talent, professionals, and families.
    

    <h2>Transitioning from Student Visa to Residency</h2>
    <p>To begin accumulating time towards long-term residency or citizenship, it is necessary to <strong>modify your legal status</strong> from «estancia por estudios» to a residence-based authorization (such as a work permit, self-employment authorization, or family reunification residence permit). Only from the moment a residence authorization is granted does time in Spain count fully as legal residence for long-term residence and nationality purposes. Residence authorizations offer broader rights, including access to social protections, movement within the Schengen area without reapplication, and full inclusion in formal labour markets.</p>

    <h2>The Impact on Your Timeline</h2>
    <p>For those seeking <strong>Spanish Citizenship</strong>, time spent as a student is entirely excluded. You must transition to a residency permit to «start the clock» for nationality. However, for <strong>EU Long-Term Residency</strong>, your study years are a strategic asset, providing a 50% credit toward your 5-year goal. In practice, this means that if you spend two years on a student stay and then convert to a residence permit, those two years may contribute one year towards the long-term residency requirement.</p>

    <h2>Common Questions: Does a student visa count towards residency in Spain?</h2>
    
    <details>
        <summary>Does a student visa count towards residency in Spain for citizenship?</summary>
        
            No. Under Article 22 of the Civil Code, «estancia por estudios» is not counted as legal residence. You must transition to a residence permit to begin the 10-year countdown for Spanish nationality.
        
    </details>

    <details>
        <summary>How much of my student time counts for EU Long-Term residency?</summary>
        
            Per Article 148 of Royal Decree 557/2011, you can count 50% of the duration of your student stays toward the 5-year requirement for the EU Long-Term Residence permit if you hold a qualifying residence permit at the time of application.
        
    </details>

    <details>
        <summary>Can I work 30 hours per week as a student in 2026?</summary>
        
            Yes. Since the 2025 Reloex reform entered into force on 20 May 2025, students can work up to 30 hours per week, provided the activity remains compatible with their studies.
        
    </details>
