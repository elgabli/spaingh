                <h3>International Income and Assets</h3>
                <p>There is no blanket exemption for income received abroad. Article 93.2 LIRPF treats employment income and income from qualifying entrepreneurial activities during the regime as Spanish-source. Article 114.2.a RIRPF excludes activities before relocation or after the end-of-relocation notification from that rule, without removing any tax due under Spanish-source rules. Other income requires separate classification and sourcing. <strong>Form 720</strong> is an information-reporting obligation, distinct from income taxation.</p>

            <h2>The 6-Month Window</h2>
            
                <p>The principal applicant has a maximum of <strong>six months</strong> from the activity start date recorded in Spanish Social Security registration, documentation allowing continued home-country Social Security legislation or, if registration is not compulsory, evidence of the activity start date (Article 116.1.a RIRPF). This is not 180 days or a universal visa- or arrival-based period. Family applicants follow Article 116.1.b.</p>
                <p>Late filing can prevent access to the regime. Check the start-date evidence and applicable deadline in advance; do not assume NIE delays extend it. Errors, requests for information and administrative decisions require individual assessment: not every defect is irreversible, and no remedy is guaranteed in every case.</p>

        <h3>Strategic FAQs</h3>

        

            <details>
                <summary>What requirements must I meet under the Startup Act (Law 28/2022)?</summary>
                
                    <p>You must acquire Spanish tax residence and not have been resident during the five tax periods before relocation. Article 93.1.b LIRPF covers specified employment relationships, becoming a company director and the entrepreneurial or highly qualified professional activities defined there. For directors, the related-party shareholding restriction applies where the entity is a patrimonial entity, not to every non-innovative business. The permanent-establishment condition, its statutory exceptions and the election deadline must also be satisfied.</p>
                
            </details>

            <details>
                <summary>How do Form 149 and Form 151 affect my tax return?</summary>
                
                    <p>Form 149 communicates the election. Form 151 is the annual IRPF return for taxpayers under the regime: they retain IRPF taxpayer status rather than entering ordinary non-resident taxation. The 24% rate up to €600,000 and 47% above it apply to the base in Article 93.2.e.1 LIRPF; certain savings income follows a separate scale.</p>
                
            </details>

            <details>
                <summary>Do I need to declare my assets outside of Spain (Form 720)?</summary>
                
                    <p>The AEAT states that the principal taxpayer validly applying the Article 93 LIRPF regime is not required to file Form 720. This is an information-reporting rule, not a blanket exemption from income or wealth taxes. Assess each relative's own status; family membership alone does not extend the dispensation. Form 721 and other obligations require separate verification: neither a duty nor an exemption is inferred from the Form 720 treatment.</p>
                
            </details>
