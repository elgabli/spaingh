        <h2>1. The Mechanics of Model 149 Filing</h2>
        <p>The expression <strong>Beckham Law 180-day rule</strong> should not be used to calculate the deadline. Article 116.1.a RIRPF gives the principal six months from the activity start date recorded in Spanish Social Security registration, documentation allowing continued home-country Social Security legislation or, if registration is not compulsory, evidence of the activity start date. Model 149 communicates the election electronically to the AEAT.</p>
    

    
        <h2>2. Why the Six-Month Deadline Matters</h2>
        <p>Late exercise of the option can prevent access to the regime. However, a missed deadline, an inaccurate date and a request to correct documentation are different issues. Their consequences and any available remedy must be assessed against the specific act and procedure. Do <strong>not</strong> assume an extension for NIE or certificate delays, or that every error is irreversible.</p>
    

    
        <h2>3. Advanced Scenarios: The «Double Alta» Trap</h2>
        <p>Previous Social Security registration and previous tax residence are different questions. Article 93 requires non-residence during the five tax periods preceding relocation; Article 116 ties the election to the documented start of the relevant activity. An old registration does not, by itself, establish either previous tax residence or the start date for a new qualifying relocation.</p>
        <p><strong>Inter-Company Transfers:</strong> Where home-country Social Security legislation continues to apply, identify the activity start recorded in the supporting <strong>coverage documentation</strong>, not merely its issue date. Apply the six-month period in Article 116.1.a and verify the actual expiry date.</p>
    

    
        <h2>4. Documentary Evidence &#038; The «Vida Laboral»</h2>
        <p>To secure approval, your filing must include proactive evidence. In 2026, we focus on three pillars:</p>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Official Social Security Extract:</strong> Cross-referencing the «Fecha de Efecto» of your Alta.</li>
            <li><strong>The Nexo Clause:</strong> An employment contract explicitly linking your move to the Spanish role.</li>
            <li><strong>Immigration documents:</strong> A UGE resolution, visa or TIE does not universally start the tax-election period. Identify the activity-start evidence required by Article 116.1.a.</li>
        </ul>
    

    
        <h2>5. The Satellite Window: Family Extensions</h2>
        <p>The spouse, children under 25 or of any age with a disability and, without a marital bond, the parent of those children may qualify under Article 93.3. They may move with the principal or later before the end of the principal&#8217;s first tax period under the regime. Article 113.3 RIRPF also permits an earlier move if they do not acquire tax residence before the principal&#8217;s first tax period under the regime. They must acquire Spanish tax residence and meet the prior five-period non-residence and permanent-establishment conditions. Their combined bases liquidables must be lower than the principal&#8217;s in each period. <strong>Each member makes an individual Model 149 election</strong> within six months of entering Spain or within the principal&#8217;s deadline if longer (Article 116.1.b). There is no universal 12-month relocation rule. Article 7.1 of Order HFP/1338/2023 requires the principal&#8217;s communication to be filed before the relatives&#8217; communications; that sequence does not itself require waiting for a favourable decision.</p>
