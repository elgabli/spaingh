<p><strong>Quick answer.</strong> The Highly Qualified Professional (HQP) permit lets a company in Spain hire a foreign executive or graduate with a decision in <strong>20 working days</strong> from the UGE-CE, family included and spouse allowed to work. In 2026 the salary must be at least <strong>€54,142</strong> for managers and directors or <strong>€40,077</strong> for graduates and technical profiles; the EU Blue Card variant requires 1.4 × the INE average salary — <strong>€39,270–41,356</strong> depending on the survey in force (0.8 × for shortage occupations and recent graduates). Base salary only — bonuses and stock options do not count. Below: who qualifies, employer requirements, documents, the step-by-step process, costs, the Blue Card comparison and the most common reasons for refusal.</p>

<h2 id="requirements">Who qualifies: the three pillars</h2>
<p>The permit (article 71 of Law 14/2013, the "Entrepreneurs Law") is designed for talent hired by companies in Spain. Three things are assessed:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The candidate</strong>: a university degree (bachelor's, master's or PhD) <em>or</em> at least three years of professional experience equivalent to the role. Any nationality outside the EU/EEA/Switzerland.</li>
<li><strong>The job offer</strong>: a contract of at least one year with a salary above the threshold for the group (below), for a position that matches the candidate's qualification.</li>
<li><strong>The employer</strong>: a company registered and up to date with tax and Social Security in Spain. Large companies and strategic groups are processed directly; SMEs and start-ups qualify when the role is strategic or the company is in a growth or innovation sector.</li>
</ol>
<p>A clean criminal record for the last five years and public or private health coverage complete the file.</p>

<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>Group 1</strong> – directors and managers</td><td><strong>€54,142</strong></td><td>HQP national route</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Group 2</strong> – scientific, intellectual and technical professionals (graduates)</td><td><strong>€40,077</strong></td><td>HQP national route</td></tr>
<tr><td>EU Blue Card – general</td><td><strong>€39,269.92</strong> (INE 2023 survey) → ≈ €41,356 once the INE 2024 survey applies</td><td>1.4 × average gross annual salary (Order PJC/44/2026); updated one month after each INE publication</td></tr>

</tbody>
</table>
<p>Only <strong>fixed base salary</strong> counts. Variable pay, commissions, bonuses and stock options are excluded: an offer of €45,000 base plus a €15,000 bonus is read by the UGE as €45,000. Salaries are gross annual figures stated in the employment contract; part-time contracts are not accepted for this permit.</p>

<h2 id="company">The employer: what the company must show</h2>
<ul>
<li>Registration in Spain (CIF), tax and Social Security certificates showing no debts.</li>
<li>A description of the position, the reason the candidate is needed and why the role is highly qualified.</li>
<li>For SMEs and start-ups: evidence that the company operates in a strategic sector (technology, R&amp;D, renewable energy, health, finance…) or of the strategic nature of the project. The UGE also accepts companies that are part of an international group.</li>
<li>Signed contract or binding offer with salary, duration (≥ 1 year) and start date.</li>
</ul>
<p>Can you be hired by your own company? Only if the company has real substance (activity, premises, other employees or investment); the UGE reviews these cases carefully and often prefers the <a href="/en/services/digital-nomad-visa-spain/">Digital Nomad</a> or entrepreneur routes.</p>

<h2 id="documents">Documents</h2>
<ul>
<li>Full passport (valid for the whole period).</li>
<li>Degree certificate, legalised or apostilled and translated — or proof of 3 years' experience (contracts, references, Social Security records).</li>
<li>Employment contract or offer letter meeting the salary threshold.</li>
<li>Criminal record certificate from the countries of residence in the last 5 years, apostilled and translated.</li>
<li>Proof of health coverage (Spanish Social Security registration or private insurance until then).</li>
<li>Company documents: CIF, tax and Social Security certificates, description of the role, power of attorney for the representative filing the application.</li>
<li>Family members: marriage/birth certificates, apostilled and translated, and proof of means for them.</li>
</ul>
<p>Order the criminal record certificate and apostilles last: they expire in 3–6 months.</p>

<h2 id="processing">Process and timeline</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Preliminary check (1 week)</strong>: role, salary group, company category, alternatives if the profile fits better elsewhere.</li>
<li><strong>File preparation (2–3 weeks)</strong>: documents, apostilles, sworn translations.</li>
<li><strong>Online submission to the UGE-CE</strong> (Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos) by the employer or its representative. If the candidate is legally in Spain (for example within the 90 tourist days), the residence authorisation is requested directly; if abroad, the authorisation is granted first and then the visa is issued at the consulate in a few days.</li>
<li><strong>Decision in 20 working days</strong>. Positive administrative silence applies: no answer within the deadline means the application is approved.</li>
<li><strong>Entry and registration</strong>: Social Security registration by the employer, fingerprint appointment and TIE card (3–6 weeks), <em>padrón</em> at the town hall.</li>
</ol>
<p>Total time from first contact to starting work: typically <strong>6–10 weeks</strong>, the fastest of Spain's work permits.</p>

<h2 id="family">Family members</h2>
<p>Spouse or registered partner, children under 18 (or dependent adult children) and dependent parents can be included in the same application or join later. They receive a residence card of the same duration and <strong>the spouse may work</strong> in Spain, employed or self-employed, without a separate permit.</p>

<p>Both are filed by the employer through the same unit. The Blue Card suits candidates who may later move within the EU; the national HQP permit is simpler when the career is in Spain.</p>

<h2 id="hqp-vs-dnv">HQP vs. Digital Nomad Visa</h2>
<p>The HQP permit is for people <strong>employed by a company in Spain</strong>. The <a href="/en/digital-nomad-visa-eligibility-checker/">Digital Nomad Visa</a> is for people who keep working for <strong>foreign</strong> companies or clients from Spain. If you are relocating with your current non-Spanish employer, the Digital Nomad route usually fits; if a Spanish company (or the Spanish subsidiary of your group) hires you, HQP. Both allow the <a href="/en/beckham-law-calculator/">Beckham Law</a> 24% tax regime if you were not a Spanish tax resident in the previous five years.</p>

<h2 id="renewal">Renewal, long-term residence and citizenship</h2>
<p>The initial authorisation lasts up to three years (or the length of the contract plus a margin) and is renewed for two years while the job continues. Changing employer is possible with a new application. After five years of legal residence you can apply for <strong>long-term residence</strong>, and after ten for <a href="/en/how-to-get-spanish-citizenship/">Spanish citizenship</a> (two years for nationals of Ibero-American countries and some others).</p>

<h2 id="refusals">Why applications are refused</h2>
<ul>
<li>Salary below the threshold once variable pay is excluded.</li>
<li>Role that does not match the qualification (a graduate hired for a non-qualified position).</li>
<li>Company with tax or Social Security debts, or without substance.</li>
<li>Expired criminal record certificates or missing apostilles.</li>
<li>Candidate who overstayed a previous tourist stay in Schengen.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>How long does the Highly Qualified Professional visa take in Spain?</h3>
<p>The UGE-CE decides within 20 working days of a complete application. Preparing the file takes 2–3 weeks, and the TIE card 3–6 weeks after arrival. Most candidates start work within 6–10 weeks.</p>
<h3>What is the minimum salary for the HQP visa in 2026?</h3>
<p>€54,142 gross per year for managers and directors, €40,077 for graduates and technical professionals. The EU Blue Card requires 1.4 × the INE average salary (≈ €39,270–41,356; 0.8 × for shortage occupations). Only base salary counts.</p>
<h3>Can I apply from within Spain?</h3>
<p>Yes, if you are legally in Spain (for example as a tourist within the 90 days). The employer files with the UGE and you receive the authorisation without leaving; you then apply for the TIE card.</p>
<h3>Can my spouse work in Spain?</h3>
<p>Yes. Family members admitted with an HQP holder receive a residence permit that allows them to work, employed or self-employed.</p>
<h3>Do I need to speak Spanish?</h3>
<p>No. There is no language requirement for the permit. Spanish is only required years later for citizenship.</p>
<h3>Can I change jobs with an HQP permit?</h3>
<p>Yes, but the new employer must file a new application meeting the same conditions. The permit is tied to a qualifying position, not to a single company for life.</p>
<h3>Does the HQP permit lead to permanent residence?</h3>
<p>Yes. After five years of continuous legal residence you can apply for long-term residence; the years also count towards Spanish citizenship.</p>
<h3>Can a start-up or a small company sponsor an HQP visa?</h3>
<p>Yes, if the position is highly qualified and the company operates in a strategic or innovative sector or belongs to an international group. The UGE looks at the substance of the company, not only its size.</p>
